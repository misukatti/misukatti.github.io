Dividing a map into rooms

A map imported from TrenchBroom comes in as one room: a single air brush carving out its whole inside. Sound fills it as one space, and no door has an opening to close. This page divides it, first with a command and then by hand where the command was wrong.

Read Overlap to extend, abut to divide first. Everything here is that rule applied.

Divide it with the command

  1. Select the map's air brush. In a 2D view, double-click inside the map and choose Room (brush …) from the list. Its outline is the size of the whole map.
  2. Choose Selection → Divide into Rooms.

The brush is replaced by several air brushes, which stay selected. The message log says how many rooms it made. Cmd Z puts the one brush back.

The command divides:

At When
A doorway or a window most of its edge is the wall it is cut through, and that wall is larger than the hole
A door the door closes the opening, whatever its size, or fills a passage with no wall round it

It does not divide:

  • at an arch across a corridor, whose frame is smaller than its opening;
  • at a step, a ledge or a room's open end;
  • where a corridor narrows between sloping walls;
  • across a door whose two sides also meet some other way round.

The space through a doorway's depth goes to the room it opens widest onto. A closet behind a door stays a room of its own.

Each room is one or more air brushes that overlap one another, so they are one space. Two rooms meet face to face at the opening between them, so each opening is a portal. The brushes take the original's place in the order, so they carve exactly what it did: nothing in the map changes shape.

The command works on any air brush that runs through several rooms, not only an imported one.

Tip: If your game sets up its importer to divide maps, Project → Tools → Import .map File… has already done this.

Check the result

Look at For
The 2D views portal lines where two rooms meet. A doorway with no line is not divided.
Audio Portals on the toolbar every opening drawn in the 3D view, if your game has audio installed. See Doors and openings.
The Problems panel rooms that nearly touch or overlap ambiguously

Fly through the level with the 2D views on a slice at head height, and look at each doorway in turn.

Divide where it did not

To divide a room at a doorway the command missed, cut its air brushes there.

  1. Select every air brush of the room that runs through the doorway. Double-click the doorway in a 2D view: each air brush there is listed as Room (brush …). Choose one, and Cmd-click to add the next.
  2. Press X for the clip tool.
  3. Hold Alt and click the face of the wall the doorway is cut through. The cut runs along that wall's plane.
  4. Press Space until the left panel says keeping both sides.
  5. Press Enter.

The two halves abut on the wall's plane, so the doorway is now an opening. Both halves keep their place in the order.

Warning: The cut runs across the whole of each brush cut, not just the doorway. Wherever else the room crosses that plane, it is divided there too. Check the 2D views for new portal lines away from the doorway, and join the room back there.

If one of the room's brushes runs through the doorway and is left uncut, it overlaps both halves and makes them one space again. Cut every brush of the room that crosses the plane.

Join where it divided wrongly

To make two rooms one space, overlap them.

  1. Press Tab until Volume on the toolbar says Air.
  2. Draw a small air brush across the opening between the two rooms, reaching into both. It only has to overlap each room by a few units.
  3. Choose Edit → Send to Back (Cmd Page Down).

Sent to the back of the order, the brush comes before every solid of the imported map. In an imported map all the rock is solid brushes, so wherever the brush reaches into rock, the rock is filled back in over it. It carves nothing; it only joins the two rooms. In a map built in Hammerite, where most rock is not brushes at all, keep the joining brush inside the two rooms.

Do this wherever a clip divided a room it should not have, and wherever the command divided at a doorway you want open.

Tip: Stretching one room's brush into the other with a face drag joins them too. That face usually runs right across the room, so it can join more than you meant. A small brush sent to the back joins exactly where you put it.

Name the doorways

A door finds the opening it fills by its name. Name each doorway a door stands in with the Audio Portals tool. See Naming an opening.

Divide and join first, then name. A cut that makes a new opening between two rooms can move a name to a different opening. See Rooms and portals.

Bake once the rooms are settled. See Lighting.

For the developer

Selection → Divide into Rooms uses a HammeriteRoomDivider with its defaults. To divide every imported map as it comes in, or to change what counts as a doorway, see Importing .map files, docs/guides/importing-map-files.md in the Hammerite package.
