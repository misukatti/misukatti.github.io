Editing

Edits go into one undo history: Cmd Z undoes, Cmd Shift Z redoes. Tools that take several steps undo their own steps while running, and add one step to the history when you finish.

A tool other than Select owns the plain letter keys while it runs. Press Esc to leave it before pressing another tool's key.

Move, rotate and scale

  1. Select brushes or entities.
  2. Press G to move, R to rotate or S to scale. The selection follows the mouse.
  3. Press X, Y or Z to lock to that axis. Press it again to unlock.
  4. Click to drop it, or press Esc to put it back.

Rotation snaps to 15°. Hold Shift for 45°, Ctrl for 5°. Alt-click moves the point it turns around.

Scaling stretches the selection about its centre, along the axes of the view you drag in, by the grid: drag 64 to the right and a room 256 across becomes 384, 64 wider on each side. Alt-click moves the point it stretches from. A prefab instance is scaled by editing its prefab.

Moving or rotating a room moves its textures with it while UV lock is on. See Texturing.

Point entities that turn show a handle at the tip of an arrow when selected. Drag the handle to turn the entity.

Dragging past the edge of a view

While you move, rotate, scale, extrude, push faces, drag vertices or a handle, drag a selection box, or resize or move a brush you are drawing, the cursor stays in the view you began in. Push it against the edge of a 2D view and the view scrolls, so the drag goes on into what was off screen. In the 3D view the cursor comes back in at the opposite edge and the drag carries on.

Copy, paste, duplicate and delete

  • Cmd D duplicates the selection and starts moving the copy. Click to drop it.
  • Cmd C copies the selection, and Cmd V pastes it where it was copied from. Cmd Alt V pastes it on the working plane under the cursor, snapped to the grid. The copy is text on the system clipboard, so it pastes into another map too. What is pasted lands on the active layer, in new groups; a prefab instance arrives as plain brushes and entities.
  • Backspace deletes the selection.

Mirror

Selection → Mirror on X, Y or Z flips the selection through its own middle. Entities in it move with the brushwork. Use it to make a left-handed copy of a doorway.

Clip

Cut brushes in two along a plane.

  1. Select the brushes to cut.
  2. Press X.
  3. Set the plane. - Drag along a surface: the cut runs along the line you drew, square to that surface, and previews as you drag. - Or click three points on surfaces: the plane passes through all three, and is made when you place the third. A point in line with the first two is refused, since it makes no plane. - Or Alt-click a face to cut along that face's plane.
  4. Press Space to choose what to keep: both sides, the side below, or the side above. The foot of the left panel says which, and what is cut away is outlined in red.
  5. Press Enter to cut, or Esc to cancel.

Hold Alt to highlight the face a plane would be taken from.

With WYSIWYG Tool Previews on, the world shows the cut as it will be. With them off, the world keeps its shape until you press Enter, and the pieces kept are outlined instead.

Extrude and push faces

Pull a face out into a new brush, or push it into its own.

  1. Hold Q and click a face to select it.
  2. Press E to extrude, or G to move the face.
  3. Move the mouse. The distance shows on the face.
  4. Click or press Enter to finish; Esc cancels.

Or do it in one gesture from the Select tool: in face mode, drag a face to move it, or hold Cmd and drag to extrude it. Hold Alt as well to take the backface, the face facing away from you. Let go of the mouse to finish.

Extruding outward adds a brush. Extruding inward cuts the face's own brush back.

In a 2D view a face moves wherever the mouse takes it. What it moves along its normal pushes it out or in; what it moves across its own plane slides it, the faces around it tilting to follow while the far side of the brush stays where it is, so a wall leans or a doorway's side slants. X, Y or Z locks the direction. In the 3D view a face moves along its normal only.

An angled face's normal is seldom along an axis, so in a 2D view press N to move faces along their normals only. A face also moves this way when another selected face is on the same brush.

When extruding or moving along normals with several faces selected, Space switches between moving them all the same distance (uniform) and each along its own direction (projected).

Sweep faces round a bend

Sweep faces out of their brushes round a curve, a new brush per segment: a corridor or an arch turning a corner, a pipe along it.

  1. Hold Q and click the faces to sweep. They must lie in one plane and face the same way.
  2. Press Shift E. The bend is shown at once, its pivot drawn in orange.
  3. Set Angle, Radius and Segments in the tool's panel. Left / Right take a segment away or add one. Space or Turn 90° turns which way the bend goes.
  4. Press Enter or Sweep to keep it; Esc cancels.

The radius is from the pivot to the middle of the selected faces, so a quarter turn of radius 128 ends 128 across and 128 on, on the grid. A radius less than half the selection's width is raised to it, which puts the inside of the bend on the pivot.

Every segment is the tier of the brush its face came from. Sweep the end of an air arch together with a detail pipe running along its ceiling, and the arch's bend is air and the pipe's detail. Air segments overlap each other a little, so the bend is one room, and the first abuts the face it was swept from, as an extrude does.

Smooth draws the walls the bend curves as one surface. A wall square to the pivot, such as the floor of a bend that turns left or right, stays flat.

Sweep faces along a path

The path sweep takes the selected faces along a smooth path through points you place: a winding tunnel, a pipe that climbs and turns, a passage that twists.

  1. Select the faces as for a sweep, and press Ctrl E. The path starts straight out from them with one point.
  2. Drag a point to move it, or press G to move the active one with the mouse and click to drop it. X, Y or Z locks the move to an axis.
  3. Drag one of the active point's three rings to turn it about that ring's axis, or press R to turn it about the view and X, Y or Z to turn it about its own axis instead.
  4. N adds a point after the last, continuing as the last one went; Backspace removes the active one. Left / Right, or clicking a point, picks another.
  5. Press Enter or Sweep to keep it; Esc cancels. While moving or turning a point, Esc puts it back.

While the tool runs, Cmd Z and Cmd Shift Z undo and redo its own edits: a point moved, turned, added or removed, or a value typed into the panel. A whole drag is one step. Keeping the sweep is one step of the map's history.

The path is shown as brushes, not carved into the world, even with WYSIWYG Tool Previews on: a path is many overlapping segments, rebuilt at every step of a drag. It is carved once you keep it.

Each point is placed and turned from the one before it, so moving or turning one carries every point after it. The panel shows the active point's Across, Up and Out from the one before, and its Pitch, Yaw and Twist. The path leaves each point the way that point faces, so turning a point steers the path through it; twisting it turns the faces about the path.

Segment length is the longest a segment may be. Each span between two points is cut into as few even segments as keep under it, so points close together and far apart make segments much alike.

Segments keep the tier of the brush each face came from, and air segments overlap so the path is one room, as in a sweep round a bend.

Vertices

  1. Select brushes.
  2. Press V. Their corners show as handles.
  3. Click a corner to select it; Cmd-click to add more. In a 2D view, drag a box to select several.
  4. Drag a selected corner to move it, or press G to move them with the mouse, X / Y / Z to lock an axis, and click to drop them.
  5. Press Enter to keep the changes.

A brush pushed out of shape is split into convex pieces. Esc deselects the corners; Esc with none selected throws away every change and leaves the tool.

Edges

The Edge tool is the Vertex tool for edges: Shift V, with brushes selected. Each edge has a handle at its middle; picking an edge picks both its ends, and they move together. In a 2D view, a box picks the edges lying wholly inside it.

Cmd-click an edge to add it, and again to take it out - its ends both go, even one another edge picked shares. Moving, Enter and Esc are as in the Vertex tool.

Merge

Select two or more brushes and press , to replace them with one brush wrapping them all. Cmd , keeps the originals.

The merged brush is convex, so it fills any gaps between the originals.

Bridge faces

Select faces and press Cmd F to build one brush spanning them.

Change a brush's tier

Choose a tier on the toolbar, select the brushes, and press U. See Building.

Grid and snapping

Moves, corners and new brushes snap to the grid. The status bar shows its size as Grid with − and + buttons; = makes it coarser and - finer, even while a tool is running.

While dragging, hold Shift to snap to twice the grid, Ctrl to a quarter.

Selection → Snap to Grid puts each selected brush and entity back on the grid, one by one.

Ctrl G shows or hides the grid on surfaces. 3D Grid in the 3D view's View dropdown shows a grid plane in that view.
