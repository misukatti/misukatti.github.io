Coming from TrenchBroom

Most of what your hands know carries over: the four views, the grid, brushes cut with a clip tool, vertices dragged, textures lined up face by face. What changes is what a brush is. This page is what to unlearn first, and where the things you reach for are.

The world is carved, not built

In TrenchBroom the world is empty space, every brush is solid, and a room is the gap you leave between walls you build. Hammerite is the other way round: everything is rock, and you draw the room.

In TrenchBroom you In Hammerite you
build six walls round a space draw one Air brush the size of the space
make a doorway by building a wall in two halves draw a small Air brush between two rooms, touching both
put a pillar in a hall draw a Solid brush in it, after the hall
mark trim and furniture func_detail draw it in Detail

So a room is one brush, not six, and its walls are its faces seen from inside. Texture the room by texturing that brush. Tab cycles the tier new brushes are drawn in; it starts on Air. The model in full is The carved world, and it is worth the five minutes.

No leaks, no sealing

Outside every room is rock, so there is no void for the map to leak into and nothing to seal. There are no gaps to hunt for either: wherever nothing is carved there is wall. The one way to see out of a room is a see-through texture on its own wall, and the Problems panel marks that.

No compiling

There is no qbsp, vis or light step to run. Every edit recompiles what it touched as you make it, and rooms, openings and visibility are always up to date. Lighting is the one thing you ask for: Cmd L and Bake. If the world ever looks wrong in a way no edit explains, Tools → Recompile Whole Level carves it all again.

Rooms are what sound and sight follow

How two Air brushes meet decides whether they are one room or two:

  • Overlap them to make one space: an L-shaped hall from two boxes, an alcove.
  • Abut them - faces touching on one plane - to make two rooms with an opening between, which a door can close and sound passes through.

There is no hint brush or area portal to place: the rooms are the portals. See Rooms and portals.

The camera works as you expect: hold the right mouse button to look round and fly with W A S D. In the flat views, drag a ruler with the right mouse button to cut the view at a height, so the floor you are on is not buried under the ones above. The whole list is in Keyboard reference.

A few things work differently:

  • A tool owns the plain keys while it runs. Press Esc to leave it before another tool's key.
  • A selection box in the 3D view takes brushes wholly inside it, and selects objects only: in face mode a drag there moves the face. Box faces in the flat views.
  • Curved patches are not brushes here. For an arch or a bend, use the Arch brush, or sweep faces round a curve or along a path (Shift E, Ctrl E).

Bringing a map across

In the Godot editor, Project → Tools → Import .map File… brings a .map from TrenchBroom, J.A.C.K. or the Radiants into the project as a Hammerite map: the void round it is filled back in as rock, every brush stays as you drew it, your layers and groups come with it, and every face keeps where its texture lay. Two things to do after:

  • The map's inside comes in as one room. Select it and choose Selection → Divide into Rooms to divide it at its doorways, windows and doors, and fix what it missed by hand: see Dividing a map into rooms.
  • Check the Problems panel, and bake.

The import is one way: Hammerite does not write .map files, so carry on in Hammerite once a map is across. See Map files.

For the developer

Your FGD is ported once into the game's own entity types, from the Godot editor's Project → Tools → Port FGD to Hammerite…, and the inspector shows its choices, flags and targets as TrenchBroom did. func_godot entity scripts run on them unchanged; func_godot built your levels in metres and Hammerite keeps the map's units, so the game's own numbers are scaled once, and it laid them out turned a quarter round from Hammerite, which matters to a script that moves things in the world's own directions. All of it is in the guide Importing .map files, docs/guides/importing-map-files.md in the Hammerite package, which the game's programmer will want.
