Performance and limits

Hammerite is developed against a large imported reference level, and each slow path found on it is fixed and the measurement recorded in the commit that fixed it. The figures below are that level's, on an Apple Silicon Mac, and move as the work goes on. Measure your own levels with the benchmarks at the end.

The reference level
brushes 7,240
rooms about 1,800
walls after carving about 130,000
entities 2,200
lightmap patches about 4 million

What it costs
Opening, cold the world is carved from scratch: about four seconds on the reference level, after a second and a half reading the file. Saving writes a worldrep cache beside the map (level.worldrep.hcache), and opening with one is faster
An edit recompiles only what the edit's carves reach, and the views draw again only what it changed: on the reference level, moving a small air brush is shown in the editor in about a tenth of a second and a detail brush in about a thirtieth, and on a small level there is no pause to notice
Drawing, in the game each room's walls are drawn only when the camera can see into it through the doorways, so the cost follows what is in view rather than the level's size
Navigation building walkable ground and links takes over a minute on the reference level (#114); it runs on the compile's worker thread
The preview in Godot's editor a HammeriteLevel shows its map's world in Godot's 3D view, unlit, from geometry kept beside the map as it is saved (level.preview.res). On City of Thieves' into_the_odd that file is 2.5 MB, written in 0.2 s; the scene opens at once and the whole map is shown 0.22 s later, its 55 chunks drawn at 0.8 ms of the CPU a frame. With no file, or one older than the map, the map is read and the geometry built on the worker pool, 8 s there, the editor never waiting on it, and kept for next time. A level's Preview In Editor turns it off
Scenes as types HammeriteSceneEntitySet builds each scene once as it is added, to read its root's exports. Over City of Thieves' 42 scenes, whole levels and test rigs among them, that took 1.1 s the first time, nearly all of it loading the scenes' resources, which placing their entities would load anyway; a median scene took 12 ms. With the resources loaded, all 42 took 0.1 s
Baking 22 minutes on the reference level on a GTX 1070 Ti, measured with 1.x and its native kernel, nearly all of it light transfer (#125). Patch data is about 1.2 GB there

These costs grow with the level. A level of tens of rooms carves in a fraction of a second and edits without a pause.

Keeping a level fast

  • Save as .hmap, and keep the worldrep cache with it. It is what makes opening fast; deleting it is safe but costs a full carve on the next open.
  • Overlap to extend a space, abut to divide one. Every air brush is a cell, and every pair that touches is a portal to find: dressing carved with many small air brushes costs more than the same shape in detail brushes.
  • Detail brushes for dressing. They do not take part in carving, so they cost nothing at compile time beyond their own faces.
  • Lightmap scale. A face's lightmap_scale (16 units a texel by default) sets how many patches it makes. Coarser on large, dim surfaces is the biggest lever on bake time and memory.

Measuring

The benchmarks print timings rather than asserting them. Run one against the tests project:

godot --headless --path tests --script res://benchmark_compile.gd   # carving, by level size
godot --headless --path tests --script res://benchmark_detail.gd    # the cost of carving dressing
godot --headless --path tests --script res://benchmark_nav.gd       # navigation build
godot --headless --path tests --script res://benchmark_persist.gd   # save and load
godot --headless --path tests --script res://benchmark_edit_map.gd -- level.hmap   # an edit on your level
godot --headless --path tests --script res://benchmark_read_map.gd -- level.hmap   # reading your level

benchmark_edit_map.gd loads a level of your own and moves a detail brush, a small air brush and a small solid there and back, printing what each recompile costs; --cold also carves the level from nothing first, as opening it without its worldrep cache does.

benchmark_read_map.gd reads a level of your own as it is and saved again in the current format, and leaves the level as it was.
